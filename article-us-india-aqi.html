US vs India AQI: Why the Same Air Gets Two Numbers

Published by Eco Tool Hub Editorial Team • October 17, 2026 • 6 min read

🌬️ Quick Answer

There is no single worldwide AQI formula. The US EPA and India's CPCB convert the same PM2.5 readings through different breakpoint tables — India's bands are wider at the top, so identical smog often scores lower (better-looking) on the Indian scale than on the US scale. Same air, two numbers, two stories.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • AQI is a translation, not a measurement: the raw measurement is PM2.5 in µg/m³ — every country translates it differently.
  • India's scale is more forgiving: what the US calls "Unhealthy" (151+), India may still label "Poor" or even "Moderate."
  • 2024 made the US stricter: the EPA tightened PM2.5 breakpoints — the "Good" band shrank from 0–12.0 to 0–9.0 µg/m³.
  • Rule of thumb: follow your local scale, but if the US number says unhealthy, believe it.

The Same Smog, Two Report Cards

Imagine a winter morning in Lahore or Delhi: a monitor reads PM2.5 = 120 µg/m³. An American app converts it to roughly US AQI 185 — Unhealthy (red). An Indian government dashboard converts the identical reading to roughly India AQI 210 — "Very Poor" (also red, but a different number from a different math). At lower pollution the gap flips direction: PM2.5 of 40 µg/m³ is about US AQI 112 but only India AQI 70. Neither is "wrong" — they are simply different rulers.

Why the Rulers Differ

Three reasons. First, health research baselines: the EPA's breakpoints come from US epidemiological studies; India's CPCB adapted them to local conditions and policy goals. Second, history: India's National Air Quality Index launched in 2014, built on older WHO guidance. Third, the 2024 EPA revision: the US tightened its PM2.5 breakpoints after new evidence on low-level exposure — India's table hasn't followed suit, so the gap widened.

Which Scale Should You Follow?

Where you live decides the official number — schools, governments, and news in India and Pakistan report the CPCB-style scale. But for your lungs, the stricter US scale is the more protective guide: if the US conversion says "unhealthy," act on it even when the local number looks milder. Frequently Asked Questions

Why does India AQI differ from US AQI?

The US (EPA) and India (CPCB) use different breakpoint tables to convert PM2.5 into AQI. India's bands are wider at the top end, so the same polluted air often scores lower on the Indian scale than on the US scale.

Which AQI scale should I follow in India or Pakistan?

Follow your local official scale for regulatory guidance, but treat the stricter US EPA number as your personal health guide — if it says unhealthy, take precautions regardless of the local figure.

What PM2.5 level is considered unhealthy?

On the US EPA scale (2024), PM2.5 above 35.4 µg/m³ is "Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" and above 55.4 µg/m³ is "Unhealthy" for everyone. On India's CPCB scale, "Poor" starts at 91 µg/m³.

Did the US change its AQI breakpoints in 2024?

Yes — the EPA tightened PM2.5 breakpoints in 2024. The "Good" band shrank from 0–12.0 to 0–9.0 µg/m³, so identical air now scores a higher (worse) AQI than before 2024.

Conclusion

Two rulers, one sky. The US and Indian AQI scales translate the same PM2.5 into different numbers because they were built from different science, different histories, and different policy choices — and the 2024 EPA revision stretched the gap further.
