          The numbers behind this calculator

          Water. Ready.gov advises storing 1 gallon per person per day for several days, covering drinking and sanitation. The UK's Be Ready campaign advises 3 litres of drinking water per person per day; we add 2 litres for cooking and basic hygiene on that standard. Children, nursing mothers and hot climates need more — when in doubt, round up. Store water in food-grade containers, keep it in a cool dark place, and replace home-filled containers every 6 months.

          Food. We plan on 2,000 kcal per adult per day and 1,600 kcal per child — the same planning figures used by established emergency food calculators. Choose foods that need no refrigeration and little or no cooking: canned meals, peanut butter, dried fruit, cereal, and ready-to-eat pouches. Remember a manual can opener. Rotate stock into everyday meals so nothing expires unused.

          Babies can't wait — plan for them first

          Adults can ration and improvise; a baby cannot. In any disruption the first shelves to empty are diapers, wipes and formula. Our rule: keep 10 diapers a day for a newborn, 8 for an older baby, one size up from what they wear today, plus wipes (roughly 20 a day), rash cream, and sealed water for mixing formula if you use it. If you breastfeed, the most important supply is food and water for the mother. Add comfort items — a familiar blanket or toy — because a calm baby in a stressful night is not a luxury, it is what lets the whole family cope.

          How long should you prepare for?

          Three days is the shared minimum across US, UK and EU guidance. Sweden advises a week, Germany 10 days. Most families build up gradually — the UK campaign's own advice — adding a day of supplies at a time.
