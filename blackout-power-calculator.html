How this calculator works

Every appliance uses energy measured in watt-hours: watts × hours used = watt-hours (Wh). Add up everything you ticked and you get your daily need. A power station never delivers its full label capacity — the inverter wastes some energy as heat — so we divide by 0.85 (85% usable), the standard planning figure used by manufacturers. The inverter size is your running watts plus a 25% safety margin; note that a fridge compressor briefly draws 3–5× its running watts when it starts, which is why we never recommend an inverter below 600W when a fridge is ticked.

A typical essentials load — fridge, five LED bulbs, WiFi router, two phones, a laptop and a fan — comes to roughly 3.2 kWh per day. That is why the ~2–3.6 kWh class is the sweet spot for one full day of backup, and why adding a space heater or kettle changes the answer dramatically: heating elements are the hungriest loads in any home.

Why plan for 3 days?

Official guidance in the US (Ready.gov / FEMA), the UK ("Be Ready" campaign) and the EU Preparedness Union Strategy all use the same starting point: a household should be able to look after itself for at least 72 hours — food, water, medicines, light, communication and a way to cook. Germany advises 10 days and Sweden a week, so the days slider goes to 7; build up gradually, a day at a time, rather than buying everything at once.

Fridge safety in an outage (USDA)

Keep the doors closed: a fridge keeps food safe for about 4 hours without power, a full freezer about 48 hours (24 hours if half full). When in doubt, throw it out.
