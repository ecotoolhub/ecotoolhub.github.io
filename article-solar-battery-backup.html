How Long Can a Solar Battery Power Your Home? Blackout Backup Guide

Published by Eco Tool Hub Editorial Team • October 14, 2026 • 7 min read

⚡ Quick Answer

A 10 kWh LiFePO4 solar battery (about 9 kWh usable) keeps essential home loads — fridge, LED lights, fans, Wi-Fi, phone charging — running for roughly 20 to 27 hours during a blackout. Add an air conditioner and that drops to 6–8 hours. Your actual backup time = usable battery capacity ÷ total watts of everything switched on.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • The simple formula: Backup hours = usable battery kWh × 1000 ÷ total load watts.
  • LiFePO4 wins: 80–90% of rated capacity is usable, versus only ~50% for lead-acid.
  • Essentials first: A fridge + lights + fans + phones draws only ~350W — a modest battery covers a full day.
  • AC is the killer: One air conditioner can consume more than all your essential loads combined.

LiFePO4 vs Lead-Acid: Usable Capacity Matters More Than the Label

A "10 kWh" battery rarely gives you 10 kWh. Lead-acid batteries should only be discharged to about 50% or their lifespan collapses — so a 10 kWh lead-acid bank gives you just 5 kWh of real backup. LiFePO4 (lithium iron phosphate) safely delivers 80–90% of its rated capacity, lasts 10+ years, and handles thousands of charge cycles. For blackout backup, usable capacity per dollar strongly favors lithium.

5 Ways to Stretch Your Battery Through a Long Blackout

  • Kill the vampires: Unplug the water heater, iron, and microwave — standby and heating loads are brutal.
  • Fan over AC: Two ceiling fans use less than one-tenth the power of an air conditioner.
  • Charge by day: With a hybrid inverter, your panels refill the battery during daylight — potentially indefinite backup in sunny weather.
  • Stagger the fridge: A full fridge stays cold for 4+ hours unopened; cycle it rather than running it nonstop.
  • LED everything: If you still have old bulbs, this is the cheapest backup-time upgrade you can make.

Can Solar Panels Recharge the Battery Mid-Blackout?

Yes — but only if your system was designed for it. Standard grid-tied inverters shut down automatically when the grid fails (a safety rule called anti-islanding, so repair crews aren't electrocuted). To keep generating during outages you need a hybrid inverter with battery backup capability, or a fully off-grid setup. If blackout resilience is your goal, confirm this with your installer before buying — it is the single most common regret we see.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long will a 10kWh solar battery power my house?

A 10 kWh LiFePO4 solar battery (about 9 kWh usable) powers essential loads like a fridge, LED lights, fans, and phone charging — roughly 300 to 400 watts — for approximately 20 to 27 hours. Running heavier loads like an air conditioner cuts that to 6 to 8 hours.

Can my solar panels charge the battery during a blackout?

Yes, but only with a hybrid or off-grid inverter. Standard grid-tied inverters shut down automatically during blackouts for safety (anti-islanding protection), so your panels stop producing until the grid returns.

How many solar batteries do I need for 24 hours of backup?

For essential loads of around 400 watts running 24 hours, you need roughly 10 to 12 kWh of usable storage — typically one 10 kWh LiFePO4 battery or two 5 kWh units. Whole-home backup including air conditioning usually needs 20 to 30 kWh.

Do solar batteries work at night?

Yes — powering your home at night is exactly what solar batteries are for. Your panels charge the battery during the day, and the stored energy runs your home after sunset or during grid outages.

Conclusion

Blackout backup is simpler — and cheaper — than most people think, as long as you size for essential loads instead of the whole house. A single 10 kWh lithium battery keeps the lights on, food cold, and phones charged for a full day. Run your own numbers through our calculator and stop guessing.
