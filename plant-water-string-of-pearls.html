How Often to Water String of Pearls: Complete Schedule & Care Guide

Published by Eco Tool Hub Editorial Team • Updated October 2026 • 6 min read

⚡ Quick Answer

Water string of pearls every 2-3 weeks, only when the soil is completely dry - it is a succulent despite its delicate look. Shallow roots mean small, infrequent drinks; overwatering turns the 'pearls' mushy and kills the plant fast.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Watering rule: Soil 100% dry = small drink. Treat it like the succulent it is.
  • Seasonal shift: Summer every 14-21 days; winter every 21-30 days.
  • Light the crown: Sunshine must hit the TOP of the pot - a dying crown with healthy trails means the top is too shaded.
  • Top killer: Overwatering. Mushy translucent pearls = rot, usually starting at the soil line.

How Often to Water String of Pearls (Senecio rowleyanus)

String of pearls fools everyone: those bead-like leaves are water-storage organs, making this trailing plant a true succulent. Owners kill it with regular houseplant watering schedules. It wants bright light on its crown, gritty fast-draining soil, and long dry spells - then it rewards you with strands that can reach several feet.

Season Watering Frequency Key Tip
Spring & Summer (growing season) Every 14-21 days Check weekly; water once 100% dry throughout (shallow roots).
Fall & Winter (dormant season) Every 21-30 days Growth slows and soil stays wet longer - reduce frequency and skip fertilizer.

Light, Soil & Water Volume for String of Pearls

Light: Bright indirect light with some direct sun hitting the top of the pot - crucial. A high shelf in a dark corner starves the crown.

Soil mix: Very gritty cactus/succulent mix in a shallow pot; deep pots stay wet at the bottom where roots never reach.

How much water: Small soak - roughly 150-250 ml for a 4-inch pot. Water lightly; the shallow root zone saturates fast.

Overwatering vs Underwatering Signs

Overwatered String of Pearls: Mushy, translucent pearls; stems turning brown and soft at the soil line; pearls dropping off.

Underwatered String of Pearls: Shriveled, dull pearls with flat spots or creases - they plump back up after a proper watering.

Root Rot Prevention Tips

  • Water in the morning and ensure the crown dries by evening - wet crowns rot overnight.
  • Bottom-watering works well: set the pot in water 10 minutes, then drain fully.
  • Propagate by laying healthy strands on moist soil - they root at every node.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are my string of pearls shriveling?

Underwatering - the pearls are spending stored water. A thorough soak should plump them within a day; if not, check for root rot.

How do I propagate string of pearls?

Lay a healthy strand on damp succulent soil, pin nodes lightly to the surface, and keep humid - roots form in 2-3 weeks.

Why is the top of my string of pearls dying?

The crown isn't getting light. Move so sun hits the soil surface, or the shaded top rots while trails survive.

Is string of pearls toxic to pets?

Yes - mildly toxic to cats and dogs. Hang it well out of reach of curious pets.

Conclusion

Watering String of Pearls (Senecio rowleyanus) comes down to one habit: check the soil before you water, and adjust for the season. Give it 100% dry throughout (shallow roots) of dry-down, a pot that drains freely, and light that matches its needs, and it will thrive for years. When in doubt, underwater slightly - most houseplants recover from thirst far faster than from rot.
