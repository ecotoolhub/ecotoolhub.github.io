Complete Guide to Carbon-to-Nitrogen (C:N) Ratio in Composting

Composting is a natural biological process that converts organic waste into nutrient-rich humus. However, to make this process happen efficiently, microorganisms need a balanced diet of Carbon (Browns) and Nitrogen (Greens).

What is the Ideal Ratio?

The golden rule of composting is maintaining a 30:1 Carbon-to-Nitrogen ratio by weight. This means for every 30 parts of carbon, you should have 1 part of nitrogen.

  • Green Materials (Nitrogen-Rich): Kitchen scraps, fresh grass clippings, coffee grounds, and vegetable peels. They provide protein and moisture.
  • Brown Materials (Carbon-Rich): Dry leaves, shredded cardboard, straw, and sawdust. They provide energy and air pockets.

What happens if the ratio is off?

If you add too much green waste, your pile will become wet, dense, and start producing a foul ammonia smell. If you add too much brown waste, decomposition will slow down drastically and nothing will happen. Use our calculator above to balance your pile instantly!
