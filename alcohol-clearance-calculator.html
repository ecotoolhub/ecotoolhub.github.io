How Alcohol Clearance Works

Your liver metabolizes alcohol at a nearly constant rate — about one standard drink per hour, or roughly 0.015% BAC per hour. Coffee, cold air, and exercise don't speed this up; only time does. This calculator uses the Widmark formula: BAC ≈ (alcohol grams ÷ (body weight × distribution ratio)) − (0.015 × hours).

Alcohol Clearance FAQs

How long does it take for alcohol to leave your system?

About one standard drink per hour. Six drinks ≈ 6+ hours from the last drink to reach zero BAC. Smaller bodies and women (lower water ratio) clear it slower.

Can coffee sober you up faster?

No. Caffeine may make you feel alert, but BAC falls at ~0.015%/hour regardless. "Wide awake but still drunk" is how bad decisions happen.

How long after drinking can I drive?

Only at 0.00% BAC to be safe — or under your country's legal limit (0.05% many countries, 0.08% US/UK). After 4–5 drinks that's typically 5–7 hours from the last drink. When in doubt, don't drive.

What counts as one standard drink?

14g of pure alcohol: 12 oz of 5% beer, 5 oz of 12% wine, or 1.5 oz of 40% spirits. Strong IPAs and generous wine pours often equal 1.5–2 drinks.
