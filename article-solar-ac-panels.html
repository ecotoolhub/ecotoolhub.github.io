How Many Solar Panels Do You Need to Run an Air Conditioner?

Published by Eco Tool Hub Editorial Team • October 19, 2026 • 8 min read

🌱 Quick Answer

A typical 1.5-ton split AC draws 1,500–1,800W while the compressor runs. In a location with 5 peak sun hours, you need roughly 5–6 × 400W panels to cover a day of cooling. A 1-ton unit needs 3–4 panels, a 2-ton unit needs 7–8. These are daytime figures — running the AC through the night needs batteries on top, which cost more than the panels and inverter combined.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Count energy, not just watts: an AC's running watts tell you the instantaneous load; the panels must cover total daily energy (watts × hours × duty cycle). That's the number that decides your panel count.
  • Inverter ACs are solar's best friend: they draw less on average, have no violent startup surge, and can cut your required panels by 20–30% versus a conventional unit of the same tonnage.
  • The surge decides the inverter: conventional compressors can briefly pull 3–5× their running watts on startup — size your inverter's surge rating for that, not the running watts.
  • Night cooling is a battery problem: 8 hours of 1.5-ton cooling overnight needs ~10–12 kWh of usable storage. Most solar-AC setups run on sunshine by day and the grid by night.

How Much Power Does an Air Conditioner Actually Use?

ACs are rated in "tons" of cooling — 1 ton = 12,000 BTU/hr. But what matters for solar sizing is the electrical input in watts, which depends on tonnage, efficiency, and whether it's an inverter (variable-speed) or conventional (fixed-speed) unit:

AC type Cooling capacity Running watts (typical) Startup surge (conventional)
Portable, 8,000 BTU~0.7 ton900–1,100W~2,500W
Window, 12,000 BTU1 ton1,100–1,400W~3,500W
Split inverter, 12,000 BTU1 ton900–1,200W~1,500W (soft start)
Split inverter, 18,000 BTU1.5 ton1,500–1,800W~2,000W (soft start)
Split conventional, 18,000 BTU1.5 ton1,700–2,000W~5,000W
Split, 24,000 BTU2 ton2,000–2,500W~7,000W

Two things jump out. First, inverter ACs barely surge — they ramp the compressor up gently, which is why they're dramatically easier to run on solar. Second, the compressor doesn't run nonstop: once the room reaches temperature, it cycles. In hot climates, budget a ~70% duty cycle — the unit draws its rated watts about 70% of the time it's switched on.

The Formula: Panels Needed for Your AC

Forget rules of thumb — the math is three steps:

Step 1 — daily energy: AC watts × hours per day × duty cycle.
Step 2 — one panel's daily output: panel wattage × your peak sun hours (use 4–6 for most sunny regions; check a solar map for your city).
Step 3 — panels needed: daily energy ÷ one panel's output, then round up and add ~20% margin.

Worked example — 1.5-ton inverter split, 8 hours/day, 5 sun hours:

Step Calculation Result
1. Daily energy1,600W × 8h × 0.7 duty cycle≈ 9,000 Wh (9 kWh)
2. Per-panel output400W × 5 sun hours2,000 Wh (2 kWh)
3. Panels9,000 ÷ 2,000 = 4.5 → round up5 panels + margin = 6

Here's the shortcut table for the common cases (400W panels, 5 peak sun hours, 8h/day, 70% duty cycle, 20% margin included):

AC size Daily energy 400W panels needed
1-ton inverter~5.5 kWh3–4
1.5-ton inverter~9 kWh5–6
1.5-ton conventional~10.5 kWh6–7
2-ton (any type)~12.5 kWh7–8

Only 4 sun hours where you live? Add roughly one more panel per row. Running the AC 12 hours instead of 8? Scale the energy — and the panels — up proportionally.

Don't Forget the Inverter (the Surge Decides)

Panels make the energy; the inverter must survive the AC's worst moment — startup. Size it two ways:

  • Continuous rating: at least 1.25× the AC's running watts. A 1.5-ton inverter AC (1,800W) wants a ≥2.25 kW inverter — a 3 kW hybrid is the sweet spot.
  • Surge rating: must exceed the compressor's startup spike. Inverter ACs barely surge (safe on most 3 kW hybrids); conventional ACs can spike 3–5×, so check the inverter's surge spec — typically 2× its continuous rating for a few seconds.

One more rule that trips people up: standard grid-tied inverters shut down during power cuts (anti-islanding protection). If blackout cooling matters to you, you need a hybrid or off-grid inverter plus batteries — our solar battery backup guide sizes that half of the system.

Night Cooling = Batteries (the Expensive Part)

Panels sleep at night, so overnight AC runs on stored energy. The math: 1.5-ton inverter × 8 night hours × 0.7 duty ≈ 9 kWh of usable storage. With LiFePO4 batteries (about 90% usable), that's roughly a 10–11 kWh battery bank — think two 5 kWh wall batteries.

Honest framing: batteries are the single most expensive component, often costing more than panels + inverter combined. That's why the most popular solar-AC setup in sunny countries is daytime solar + grid at night (or net metering, where daytime surplus offsets night usage on the bill). Go full off-grid night cooling only if outages or tariffs make it worth the battery bill.

4 Ways to Cut Your Panel Count

  • Switch to an inverter AC: 20–30% less energy than a conventional unit of the same tonnage — that's one or two fewer panels, and no surge headaches.
  • Set 26°C, not 18°C: every degree lower adds roughly 6–8% to consumption. A well-set thermostat is the cheapest "extra panel" you'll ever buy.
  • Shade and seal the room: curtains on sun-facing windows, sealed door gaps, and clean filters can drop the duty cycle from 70% toward 50% — cutting panel needs by nearly a third.
  • Pre-cool with the sun: run the AC hardest during peak solar hours (11 AM–3 PM) and let the cooled room coast into the evening, instead of starting at sunset when the panels are done.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run a 1.5-ton AC on 4 solar panels?

Four 400W panels give you 1,600W of rated capacity — barely the running draw of a 1.5-ton AC, and only in perfect midday sun. For reliable daytime cooling you need 5–6 × 400W panels: the extra capacity covers morning/evening hours, cloudy patches, and the fact that panels rarely produce their full rated wattage.

Do I need batteries to run my AC on solar?

Not for daytime-only cooling on a grid-tied or hybrid system — the panels feed the AC directly while the sun shines and the grid covers the rest. You only need batteries if you want the AC to keep running after sunset or through power cuts. Night cooling for a 1.5-ton unit needs roughly 10–12 kWh of usable battery storage, which is the most expensive part of the system.

Will a 3kW inverter run a 1.5-ton air conditioner?

Usually yes — a 3kW hybrid inverter with a surge rating of 6kW or more handles an inverter-type 1.5-ton AC comfortably, since these ramp up gently. For an older conventional (non-inverter) AC, check the surge rating carefully: the compressor can briefly pull 3–5× its running watts on startup, which can trip an undersized inverter.

Can solar panels run my AC during a power cut?

Only with the right equipment. Standard grid-tied inverters are required by law to shut down during outages (anti-islanding protection), so your panels go dead too. A hybrid inverter with battery backup keeps the AC running through blackouts — pair this guide with our solar battery backup article to size that part.

How much does a solar setup for one air conditioner cost?

For daytime-only cooling of a 1.5-ton AC, budget roughly $1,500–$2,500 for panels, a hybrid inverter, and installation — prices vary a lot by country. Adding enough battery storage for nighttime cooling typically doubles that or more, since batteries are the single most expensive component.

Conclusion

Running an AC on solar is one of the most satisfying upgrades a homeowner can make — your biggest electricity hog, fed by your cheapest energy source. The panel math is simple: daily kWh ÷ (panel watts × sun hours), plus margin. For most people that's 5–6 panels for a 1.5-ton inverter AC, a 3 kW hybrid inverter, and the grid (or batteries) for the night shift.
