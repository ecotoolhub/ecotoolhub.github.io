How Much CO2 Do You Really Produce? Calculate Your Carbon Footprint in 2 Minutes

Published by Eco Tool Hub Editorial Team • October 17, 2026 • 6 min read

🌍 Quick Answer

Your two biggest controllable CO2 sources are electricity (~0.85 kg per kWh) and driving (~0.21 kg per km). An average home using 800 kWh/month produces 680 kg of CO2 monthly from electricity alone; a 200 km weekly commute adds ~168 kg more. The global average footprint is about 4.7 tons per person per year, the US average is 15–16 tons, and the sustainable target is just 2 tons. Two numbers from your bills give you your exact figure in under two minutes.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • 1 kWh of electricity ≈ 0.85 kg CO2 — so your monthly bill is a monthly emissions bill too.
  • 1 km of driving ≈ 0.21 kg CO2 — a 200 km weekly commute is ~2 tons of CO2 per year on its own.
  • Global average: ~4.7 tons/year; US: 15–16 tons; sustainable target: ~2 tons. Most of us are 2–8× over.
  • Two numbers calculate your footprint: monthly kWh + weekly driving km. That's the whole formula.

Your Electricity Bill Is Also a Carbon Bill

Every kilowatt-hour you pull from the grid carries the carbon of however it was generated. Planning figures commonly use 0.85 kg of CO2 per kWh — the number our calculator uses too. Multiply it by your monthly usage (it's printed on every bill) and you get your electricity footprint in seconds:

Monthly Electricity Monthly CO2 Annual CO2
300 kWh (efficient apartment)255 kg~3.1 tons
600 kWh (typical apartment)510 kg~6.1 tons
900 kWh (average US home)765 kg~9.2 tons
1,500 kWh (large home, heavy AC)1,275 kg~15.3 tons

Notice something striking: a large home's electricity alone can exceed the entire global average footprint. Electricity is usually the single biggest line item most people can actually control — which is also good news, because it's the easiest to cut.

What Your Commute Really Costs the Climate

An average petrol car emits about 0.21 kg of CO2 per kilometer. Driving distance is the second input our calculator needs — weekly kilometers, times 4 weeks, times 0.21:

Weekly Driving Monthly CO2 Annual CO2
50 km (occasional driver)42 kg~0.5 tons
150 km (regular commuter)126 kg~1.5 tons
300 km (heavy commuter)252 kg~3.0 tons
600 km (road warrior)504 kg~6.0 tons

Put a typical household together — 800 kWh of electricity and 200 km of weekly driving — and you get 848 kg of CO2 per month, or about 10.2 tons per year, from just two habits. That's before food, flights, shopping, or heating fuel. This is why the averages are so high: the math is relentless.

How Many Trees Would Offset You?

A mature tree absorbs roughly 22 kg of CO2 per year. Divide your annual footprint by 22 and you get your personal forest:

  • 5 tons/year → ~227 trees
  • 10 tons/year → ~455 trees
  • 15 tons/year → ~682 trees

It's a vivid number — and it's exactly what our calculator shows you after your result. Planting trees is genuinely useful, but the honest math says reducing beats offsetting: cutting 1 ton of emissions is worth more than planting 45 trees and waiting a decade.

5 Quick Wins That Actually Move Your Number

Ranked by impact-per-effort, all verified against the calculator's own formula:

  • Kill standby and switch to LEDs: standby "vampire" load is often 5–10% of home electricity. LEDs use ~75% less than old bulbs. Drop 100 kWh/month and you cut 85 kg of CO2 monthly.
  • One car-free day per week: skipping one day of a 200 km/week commute cuts driving CO2 by ~14% — about 24 kg/month, or 0.3 tons/year, for zero cost.
  • Raise the AC 1°C / lower heating 1°C: heating and cooling are typically half of home energy. A single degree trims roughly 6–10% of that load.
  • Combine trips and drive smoothly: gentle acceleration, steady speed, and proper tire pressure cut fuel use 10–20% — the same kilometers, less CO2 per km.
  • Switch to a green electricity tariff or rooftop solar: this attacks the 0.85 factor itself. Clean electricity pushes your per-kWh emissions toward zero — the single biggest lever in the whole formula.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good carbon footprint per person?

About 2 tons of CO2 per year is the per-person target scientists cite for staying within safe climate limits. The global average is roughly 4.7 tons, the EU average about 7, and the US average 15–16 — so "good" means being well below your country's average and moving toward 2.

How much CO2 does 1 kWh of electricity produce?

It depends on the grid mix: coal-heavy grids approach 1 kg per kWh, cleaner grids sit around 0.4–0.5 kg. Carbon calculators commonly use 0.85 kg per kWh as a planning figure. At that rate, an 800 kWh monthly bill equals 680 kg of CO2 per month from electricity alone.

How much CO2 does driving produce per km?

An average petrol car emits about 0.21 kg of CO2 per kilometer. So a 200 km weekly commute (800 km/month) produces roughly 168 kg of CO2 per month — about 2 tons per year — before you count electricity, food, or flights.

How do I calculate my carbon footprint?

You need just two numbers: monthly electricity usage in kWh (from any bill) and weekly driving distance in km. Multiply kWh by 0.85 for electricity emissions, and weekly km × 4 × 0.21 for driving emissions.
